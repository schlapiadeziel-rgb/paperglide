# Genre research notes (sources preserved)

## Metal Slug (gaminghell.co.uk/MetalSlug.html)
- Inviting controls: touching enemies does NOT kill; only vehicles running you over. Generous jump physics (steer freely rising/landing).
- Slowdown when screen busy helps reaction (shmup-like).
- Special weapons reduce tapping; tank (Slug) = 3 hits, 8-way vulcan, trade-off vs mobility; jumping out gives i-frames. Players who lose the tank are never stuck: POWs dropped before bosses with grenades/weapons.
- Fairness via telegraphs: boss charges laser, tanks rear up before firing, small tanks stop and aim, soldiers take their time to stab. "Challenging but fair."
- Charm: expressive enemy soldiers: idle goof-off animations (laughing, lounging, cooking at campfire), screaming in terror at your arrival, as many goofing-off animations as violent deaths; dark-comedy tone. Player idle animations (reload, walkie-talkie).
- POWs you rescue give items (from Gunforce II). Point milking disliked; knife close-range for score.
- Infantry climb on tank, lob grenade inside, cover cannon: shake them off by wiggling.
- Animation study (resetera / YouTube "Animation in Metal Slug"): the Slug reacts like a person (droops head, jumps with legs); huge frame counts; staggered animation.

## Blazing Chrome (denofgeek.com/games/blazing-chrome-review/)
- Contra-style run/jump/dodge/shoot + melee attack + occasional mechs = Metal Slug vibe.
- Carry up to 4 weapons (grenade launcher, charge laser, energy whip); laser sword melee up close.
- Regular checkpoints; lives set by difficulty. Criticism: accidentally picking a very different weapon mid-fight is punishing -> weapon pickups must be readable/consistent.
- Strong retro soundtrack matters.

## Broforce (waltoriouswritesaboutgames.com/2018/07/29/the-joyous-destruction-of-broforce/)
- Destructible terrain -> chain reactions: destroyed block drops explosive barrel on enemies, explosion hurls them, suicide bombers go off, propane tank rockets across the pit... "Triggering these is always a joy."
- Level design trains players to destroy terrain around hard-to-hit foes (use environment as weapon).
- Enemies flail and run when explosions cascade; the binocular lookout runs around yelling to alert others (readable alert state + humor).
- Announcer screams lines; changes lines when the player struggles ("YOU CAN DO IT! ONE MORE TIME!").
- Rescued prisoners = extra lives + character swap; strong incentive to rescue.
- One hit kills -> tension; difficulty ramps from cathartic blasting to tactical.

## Huntdown / Overtime (rogueliker.com/huntdown-overtime-review/)
- Cover-based shooting, weaving between gunfire and melee thugs; slick fast pace.
- Environmental kills: blow up barrels and cars, electrify enemies by hurling fans, crush with vending machines (Broforce-like chaos).
- Melee important for closing distance/finishing; heavy vs quick melee trade-off.
- Bounty bosses with personalities and unique mechanics (sniper with crosshair you must locate; rocket boss whose homing missiles you redirect).

## Cuphead (fandom / digitaltrends)
- Parry pink objects -> fills super meter; parries raise grade; meter-driven super moves reward skillful risk.
- Boss-focused, readable patterns, grade at end.

## Game juice: "The Art of Screenshake" (Jan Willem Nijman, Vlambeer, INDIGO 2013, youtube AJdEqssNZ-U)
Techniques (in order of the talk): basic animation, sound, lower enemy HP, higher rate of fire, more enemies, bigger bullets, muzzle flash, faster bullets, less accuracy (spray), impact effects, hit animation (flash), enemy knockback, permanence (corpses, casings, debris), camera lerp, camera position (look ahead toward aim), screen shake, player knockback (recoil), sleep/hit-stop on hits, gun delay/kick, strafing, more permanence, more bass, super machine gun, random explosions, meaning, camera kick (toward shot direction), bigger explosions.
- davetech.co.uk/gamedevscreenshake: trauma-based shake that decays; stack intensity with recent hits.

## "Juice it or lose it" (Martin Jonasson & Petri Purho, GDC 2012; longwelwind.net/blog/juice-it/)
- "A juicy game feels alive and responds to everything you do: tons of cascading action and response for minimal user input."
- Tweening/easing on everything (scale pops, squash & stretch), color flashes, particles on every interaction, sound on every event, UI that bounces/wobbles, eyes/personality, screen shake, trails, permanence.
